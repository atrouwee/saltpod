# Players: the hardware behind "ripoffs are coming back too"

**Status: RESEARCHED FROM SOURCES on 2 October 2026.** Everything below is from WebSearch/WebFetch
done this session; nothing here was measured on hardware (compare `research/ROCKBOX.md`, which did
touch `/Volumes/IPOD` read-only -- this document touches nothing). Evidence ranges from
September 2024 (Tangara's crowdfund) to September 2026 (Astell&Kern's Clarus ship date); most
pricing was observed 1-2 October 2026 and is marked as such. Two sources (`rd.com`, `axios.com`)
returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch; those figures are carried from WebSearch result snippets only,
flagged inline, and should be treated as secondary. Where a number could not be found at all, it
says so rather than guessing.

This document does not cover the owned-music software landscape, the streaming-fatigue argument,
or the cultural case for physical media -- `research/MARKET.md` (written concurrently) has that.
This is hardware only, and inside hardware the one question that actually matters for this repo:
**how does music get onto each device**, because that is the only axis on which "could saltpod ever
target this" turns.

## 1. Audiophile DAPs

The high end did not stop moving. Four companies launched or re-priced flagship and
near-flagship players in the twelve months before this research, and a fifth (FiiO) opened an
entirely new budget sub-brand built on nostalgia styling.

**FiiO.** The M-series Android DAPs kept shipping on schedule: **M23** ($749, spring 2024,
AKM AK4191EQ DAC + THX-AAA 78+ amps) (<https://www.ear-fidelity.com/fiio-m23-review/>,
<https://www.notebookcheck.net/FiiO-M23-Hi-Fi-music-player-launches-with-1000-mW-THX-amplifier-and-5-5-inch-touchscreen.810209.0.html>);
**M11S** (~$500, pitched as "80% of M11 Plus performance") (<https://www.fiio.com/newsinfo/781643.html>);
**M21** (Walkman-inspired design, Snapdragon 680, quad CS43198 DAC, 950 mW output, $369.99 as of
the April 1, 2026 price rise) (<https://www.ecoustics.com/news/fiio-confirms-price-increases-starting/>);
**M33 R2R** (24-bit R2R ladder DAC, Android 13, desktop mode, 2.2 W output, shown at CES 2026
January, $699.99 effective April 1, 2026) (<https://porta-fi.com/fiio-m33-r2r-ces-2026/>,
<https://www.ecoustics.com/news/fiio-confirms-price-increases-starting/>); and **M25 / M25 R2R**
(two DAC architectures, shown at CanJam London 18-19 July 2026, tentative ~$1,000, not yet shipping
with final specs) (<https://www.ecoustics.com/products/fiio-m25-r2r-daps/>). FiiO cited chip costs,
tariffs and "AI chase" memory-price inflation for the April 2026 increases
(<https://mobileaudiophile.com/editorial/fiio-dap-price-increase-2026/>).

The retro styling the brief flagged is real and is its own sub-brand, **FiiO Snowsky**, launched
through 2026: **Echo** ($74.99 MSRP), **Echo Mini** ($49.99, "the second retro DAP FiiO has rolled
out," Walkman-styled) (<https://www.headfonia.com/fiio-snowsky-echo-mini-review/>), **Echo Nano**

14-15 March 2026, ~$52.56 via FiiO's AliExpress store)

(<https://apos.audio/products/fiio-cp13-cassette-player-transparent>,
<https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/1830410-REG/fiio_cp13t_cp13_portable_stereo_cassette.html>)
-- not a digital player at all, but direct evidence that a major Chinese audio brand now sees
"retro device, digital guts or none" as a product category worth building a whole line around.

**HiBy.** R-series DAPs are current: **R4** (Android, Class-A amp, 4-way DAC)
(<https://www.audiophile-heaven.com/2024/05/hiby-r4-hifi-android-dap-class-a-amp-4-way-dac.html>),
**R2 II** (volume wheel added over the original R2's flat button layout)

**Shanling.** Clear two-tier split: Android flagships -- **M3 Plus** ($499, 2025, Android 13, quad

**Cayin.** **N3 Pro** ($479), a non-Android, custom-RTOS DAP with USB modes limited to Mass

(<https://theheadphonelist.com/classical-conceits-a-review-of-the-cayin-n3-pro/>, device settings
confirmed via headfonics first-contact review). It has an unofficial community Rockbox port in

**iBasso.** **DX170** ($449), **DX180** ($529, now discontinued), **DX320** ($1,599)
(<https://ibasso.com/product/dx320/>, <https://ibassousa.com/ibasso-dx180/>). Android-based;

<https://www.ibasso.com/uploadfiles/download/DX180userguide.pdf>).

**Sony.** **NW-A306** (street $279-299, list $399.99) and **NW-ZX707** ($699) remain the current
A/ZX line (<https://www.inverse.com/gear/sony-walkman-nw-zx707-nw-a306-music-player>). Notably,

## 2. The budget flood -- the "ripoffs"

This is a real, distinct, and currently growing segment, not a fringe curiosity, and it deserves
the word "ripoff" literally: several of these are overt, unlicensed visual clones of specific Apple
products, sold at a fraction of even a used original's price.

**Innioasis** is the most direct hit on the brief. The **G1** is a straight click-wheel homage to
the iPod Classic -- "the closest thing to the real thing," per one reviewer -- priced $69.99 (list
$99.99, 30% off), 1500 mAh battery, 25-hour claimed playback, USB-C
(<https://www.innioasis.com/products/g1>). Reviews are mixed on build: the wheel and button
responsiveness is criticised, and the interface reportedly struggles once a large library is
loaded onto the 64 GB unit (search-result review synthesis, no single primary review URL isolated).
The **Y1** ("Classic Y2K MP3 Player," also click-wheel) is a documented TikTok Shop seller: **13.9K
units sold** at $69.99 (marked down 22% from $89.99) per the platform's own sales counter
(<https://shop.tiktok.com/us/k/mp3-million-catalogue> family of listings, TikTok Shop product
pages) -- this is one of the only *unit-count* data points in this whole document rather than a
trend-piece adjective. Innioasis's own "About" page claims the brand has "surpassed 10 million
units sold worldwide... in over 120 countries" (<https://www.innioasis.com/pages/about-us>) --
treat this as a marketing claim, not an audited figure; it is brand-lifetime and not broken out by
model or year. The G1 is Android-based (8-core SoC), supports MP3/WAV/FLAC/AAC/APE/OGG/M4A/WMA,
ships with Spotify/Pandora/Tidal/Deezer/Amazon Music preinstalled but **cannot install any other
app**, including newer versions of those same apps
(<https://manuals.plus/innioasis/innioasis-g1-mp3-player-with-bluetooth>). Transfer is plain USB
drag-and-drop into a folder on the computer, plus a companion phone-to-device transfer app; the
device runs a "one-click music scan" that builds its own library and playlists from whatever is in
the folders (<https://www.innioasis.com/a/docs/q8/music-app>).

**Surfans** sits a notch above the pure clones on build and price: **F20** (~$125, 2-inch LCD,
Bluetooth 5.2 LDAC, no Wi-Fi/no streaming apps at all) and **F28** (~$179, adds output power and
screen quality) (<https://www.hifioasis.com/reviews/surfans-f20-review/>,
<https://www.hifioasis.com/reviews/surfans-f28-review/>). The F20 runs a Linux-based, buildroot,
proprietary-but-simple firmware, presents as plain USB mass storage, and -- notably -- has an
actual community **Rockbox port** with a boot menu offering stock firmware or Rockbox
(<https://forums.rockbox.org/index.php?topic=54420.0>,
<https://forums.rockbox.org/index.php/topic,54570.0.html>).

**AGPTEK, Ruizu, Mechen, Timmkoo** are the generic-Amazon-storefront tier: clip players, touchscreen
players, kids' players, mostly $15-$100. Concrete prices found: **Ruizu X02** $20-23
(<https://www.ruizutek.com/shop/mp3player/ruizu-x02-mp3-player/>); **Mechen M30** (DAC decoding,
DSD) under $85-100 (<https://www.amazon.com/MECHEN-M30-Lossless-Resolution-High-Res/dp/B0DMF9MCDC>).
Timmkoo data was thin -- only generic category listings found, no single flagship SKU with a
stable price (Amazon storefront only, <https://www.amazon.com/Electronics-TIMMKOO/s?rh=n%3A172282%2Cp_89%3ATIMMKOO>).
All four brands' manuals describe the same mechanism: connect via USB, the device appears as a
removable disk, drag files in; several explicitly support `.m3u`/`.m3u8` playlist import, with
AGPTEK's own documentation recommending MediaMonkey on Windows as a sync helper rather than
requiring it (<https://manuals.plus/asin/B0D5H54J3Z>, AGPTEK A29/M3 manuals).

**Is the volume real or just visible?** The only hard unit number found is the TikTok Shop
13.9K-sold counter on one SKU. Everything else -- Innioasis's "10 million" lifetime claim, the
general sense that AliExpress/Temu/TikTok Shop are "full of" these -- is visibility without an
independently audited sales figure. No Circana/NPD-style retail panel data broken out for MP3
players was found (see Section 6).

## 3. Open and enthusiast hardware

**Tangara** (Cool Tech Zone, Australia) is the one genuinely open, from-scratch iPod-alternative
hardware project, and it is buyable by a normal person today. ESP32-WROVER-E main MCU + SAMD21
USB/power co-processor, 1.8" colour touch-wheel display, SDXC storage to 2 TiB, WAV/MP3/FLAC/
Opus/Vorbis decode (<https://www.cnx-software.com/2024/02/06/tangara-portable-open-source-esp32-music-player/>).
Fully funded on Crowd Supply in February 2024
(<https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2024/02/09/open-source-ipod-project-tangara-fully-funded/>);
first batch shipped to backers through 2024-2025
(<https://www.crowdsupply.com/cool-tech-zone/tangara/updates/the-first-batch-of-tangara-is-shipping>).
As of this research the Crowd Supply campaign page itself shows "Not Available," but Mouser is
confirmed to be holding the second batch in stock for ongoing sales
(<https://www.crowdsupply.com/cool-tech-zone/tangara>) -- so it is purchasable, just not through a
single obvious checkout button. Price **$249** plus $8 US / $18 worldwide shipping. Hardware,
firmware and documentation are fully open (Codeberg: <https://codeberg.org/cool-tech-zone/tangara-fw>).

Its sync model is the purest filesystem-truth design found anywhere in this research, which matters
directly for saltpod. From the project's own docs: "Tangara works with music that you own, stored
offline on an SD card" (<https://cooltech.zone/tangara/docs/music-library/>). Playlists are
literally plain-text `.m3u`/`.m3u8`/`.playlist` files on the SD card root, containing relative
filepaths one per line; the documentation's own suggested workflow for building one is `find Music
-type f > all_tracks.playlist`, piped through `grep` to filter
(<https://cooltech.zone/tangara/docs/playlists/>). Tag handling is modelled explicitly on Beets'
defaults (album artist support, "Artist, The" sort convention, disc numbers)
(<https://cooltech.zone/tangara/docs/music-library/>). Honest gap, stated by the project itself:
"Tangara currently does not handle embedded album art well" and recommends extracting cover art to
per-album folder files with Beets instead -- the opposite of most Android-based DAPs, which handle
embedded art fine and treat folder.jpg as the fallback.

**Rockbox** itself remains actively developed -- new git builds continuously, stable releases on a
roughly four-month cadence, Rockbox 4.0 (April 2025) brought the iPod Classic/Video ports to
"stable" and added 192 kHz decode
(<https://www.howtogeek.com/rockbox-4-0-custom-firmware-mp3-players-old-ipods/>,
<https://hackaday.com/2025/04/19/rockbox-4-0-released/>). This repo's own `research/ROCKBOX.md`
(2026-10-02) already covers the iPod-specific install mechanics, the `Rockpod` SSD-aware downstream
fork, and the exact `apps/playlist.c` parsing rules in detail -- not repeated here. What is new in
this document: Rockbox-adjacent porting is not limited to Apple hardware. Community ports exist for
budget hardware too -- the Surfans F20 (above) and an in-progress Cayin N3 Pro port
(<https://github.com/ctzjj/Rockbox-N3PRO>) -- meaning the "flash a better OS onto cheap hardware"
subculture spans both the enthusiast and the budget-ripoff segments; they are not as separate as
they look.

**Flash-modded iPods as a product category** are a live small-business trade, not a dead hobby.
**iFlash** boards are in production and in stock: Solo (1x SD/SDHC/SDXC) $33-39, Quad (4x microSD)
also available (<https://www.iflash.xyz/store/iflash-solo/>, multiple eBay/Amazon listings).
**Elite Obsolete Electronics** sells assembled refurbished Classics ($69.99-79.99 for 3rd-gen base
units, from $179.99 with an iFlash SD upgrade, $169.99-249.99+ for 6th/7th-gen) and, per owner
Austin Lucas, handles roughly **70 orders a day**, 70% of which are individual replacement parts
and 30% full refurbished units, with "sales seem to go up and up" year over year
(cited via <https://www.backmarket.com/en-us/c/tech-education/ipods-rise-in-popularity>, an article
whose visible "updated" date is December 18, 2024, though it continues to be the figure cited in
2026 trend pieces -- flagged as possibly stale even though still in circulation).
**PlayerMods** builds custom iPod Classics up to 2 TB starting at $259
(<https://player-mods.com/>). On Etsy, fully assembled SSD/SDXC-modded Classics range roughly
$150-$750 depending on generation and capacity (examples: $153.60 for a 2 TB SSD 5.5-gen unit,
$294.17 for a 2 TB 7th-gen with USB-C, $499 for a 2 TB SDXC unit, $707.99 for a custom Tarkan-board
build) (various Etsy listings, search-aggregated). **Yes, this is buyable by a normal person** --
arguably the most accessible of the three enthusiast categories, since it requires zero technical
skill: you are simply buying a finished device from a small business.

## 4. The iPod itself as current hardware

**Refurb market, now.** Back Market's current US listings (observed 1-2 October 2026): iPod touch
7 32GB $125.00; iPod classic 7 160GB $301.99; iPod touch 6 16GB $72.00
(<https://www.backmarket.com/en-us/l/ipod/68c8dc90-2c57-4a17-9224-23b77ab08271>). eBay refurb
listings currently range roughly $184.95-$299.95, with some bare 6th-gen 80GB units as low as
$124.99 (search-aggregated eBay listings). **Five-years-ago comparison: not found.** No 2021
baseline price for the same condition/capacity tier was located in sources checked; this is a real
gap, not an oversight -- Back Market and eBay's own historical listing data were not retrievable
through search, and the figure is worth getting directly from the owner's own eBay purchase history
if he wants it precisely.

**Growth claims (self-reported by resellers, repeated widely).** Back Market: refurbished iPod
sales "risen an average of 15.6% per year since 2022," Q4 sales "up 117% compared to the rest of
the year" (<https://www.backmarket.com/en-us/c/tech-education/ipods-rise-in-popularity>, same
staleness caveat as above). A separate figure -- "a notable 48% jump in iPod sales in 2025, over
the year before" -- appears in `rd.com`'s "iPods Are Back in Style" piece per WebSearch snippet;
direct fetch of that URL returned HTTP 403, so this number is carried at one remove and should be
independently checked before being treated as solid (<https://www.rd.com/article/ipods-back-in-style/>).

**Search-interest data.** Multiple outlets (Digital Trends, Root-Nation, Asted Cloud, originally
reported by Axios) cite eBay search volume for "classic iPod" up 25% and "iPod Nano" up 20%,
January-October 2025 versus the same months in 2024
(<https://www.digitaltrends.com/wearables/gen-z-is-fueling-an-ipod-comeback/>,
<https://root-nation.com/en/news-en/it-news-ua/en-ipod-is-making-a-comeback/>,
<https://asted.cloud/news/ipod-players-are-popular-again-sales-have-risen-by-20-25-the-reasons-for-this-growth>).
The original Axios article (<https://www.axios.com/2026/02/21/ipods-music-genz-nostalgia>) returned
HTTP 403 on direct fetch; this is secondary-sourced for the same reason as above.

**The survey behind the "Gen Z" headline.** Writer Emily White ran an actual (if small) primary
survey, published 4 December 2025: 70+ respondents who currently use an MP3 player, 32% of them
Gen Z, 40% started using their device within the past year, another 26% started 1-3 years ago (66%
total within three years), 26% now use their MP3 player *instead of* streaming, and 39% have
modified, customized, repaired or refurbished their device
(<https://emwhitenoise.substack.com/p/why-are-ipods-making-a-comeback>). The same piece notes
"vintage" iPods sold out at Urban Outfitters at $349 each -- a retail-arbitrage data point, not a
refurb-market one, worth noting since $349 for a cosmetic "vintage" relaunch unit is well above
what a modded, storage-upgraded, genuinely more useful Classic costs from PlayerMods or Elite
Obsolete Electronics (Section 3).

**Modded-Classic assembled prices**: see Section 3 -- $259 (PlayerMods) to ~$750 (fully custom,
large capacity) is the real market, not the $301.99 stock-refurb Back Market price.

## 5. The adjacent "dumb device" movement (brief)

**Light Phone III**'s "Music Tool" is the opposite design philosophy from saltpod: one playlist,
managed entirely through an online dashboard where you upload files that get converted, synced and
stored centrally (20 GB cap), not drag-and-drop onto a mounted filesystem
(<https://support.thelightphone.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048240532-Music-Tool>). Pricing has moved
from a $599 pre-order to $799 and, by August 2026, $899 unlocked
(<https://www.engadget.com/mobile/smartphones/the-minimalist-light-phone-iii-is-officially-available-for-purchase-150056138.html>,
search-aggregated pricing history). **Daylight Computer's DC-1** ($729-799, "Live Paper" e-ink
tablet) supports Spotify as an Android app but is not a dedicated music device and is included only
because the brief named it; music is incidental to its reading/writing purpose
(<https://liliputing.com/daylight-computer-dc-1-is-a-799-tablet-with-a-live-paper-display-designed-to-be-easy-on-the-eyes-but-not-the-wallet/>).
**Playdate** (Panic Inc., crank-controlled handheld) is referenced purely as the cultural adjacent
case: a small, single-purpose, nostalgically-styled device category succeeding commercially enough
to raise its price twice -- $179 at 2022 launch, $199, then $229 from 25 March (reported 2025)
citing manufacturing-cost increases from a factory move
(<https://www.engadget.com/gaming/the-diminutive-playdate-console-is-getting-a-price-increase-to-229-on-march-25-120004199.html>).
It has nothing to do with music and nothing to do with saltpod's sync problem; it is evidence only
that "small dedicated device, not a phone" is a viable product category right now across more than
one industry.

## 6. How does music get on it: the comparison that matters

This is the section the brief called most important, so it gets the most careful sourcing. The
underlying finding is **not** a clean split between "budget" and "premium," and not cleanly
"Android vs not" either -- it is the **USB transfer mode the firmware chose to implement**, and
that choice tracks neither price nor prestige. Sony, a mainstream premium brand, ships plain mass
storage. Astell&Kern, the most expensive brand in this document, ships MTP-only with no mass
storage option at all. FiiO's cheap retro SnowSky line uses MTP despite looking like a toy.

| Device | USB mode | macOS story | Index/DB | Playlists | Reads embedded tags | Album art |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **iPod Classic, stock firmware** (saltpod's target) | USB disk mode, but the *index* is proprietary | Finder sees the disk; nothing usable without writing the `iTunesDB` | **Must write `iTunesDB`** -- the computer, not the device, builds the index (`research/itunesdb-format.md`) | Proprietary binary playlist records inside `iTunesDB`, not text files | Yes, but the sync tool (saltpod) must have written them in correctly first | `ArtworkDB`, separate proprietary binary store |
| **iPod Classic + Rockbox** | USB disk mode (FAT32) | Plain drag-and-drop, no app | None required; optional on-device tag DB, otherwise pure file browser | Plain `.m3u`/`.m3u8` text, relative-path rules documented in `research/ROCKBOX.md` | Yes, native, no transcoding needed (FLAC/ALAC/WAV/Vorbis/Opus/WavPack) | Folder-based, reads embedded where supported by codec |
| **FiiO Android-OS DAPs** (M11S/M21/M23/M25/M33) | **MTP** | Needs Android File Transfer (<https://www.fiio.com/newsinfo/754182.html>) | On-device FiiO Music app scans and builds its own library; "Reset database" is a user action, not something the host writes | Reads `.m3u`/`.m3u8` found in the folder tree during scan | Yes, incl. FLAC; extracts embedded art to a generated cache folder, capped by Android's per-folder file-count limit (<https://www.blisshq.com/music-library-management-blog/2019/04/09/fiio-album-art/>) | Embedded, or `cover.jpg`/`album.jpg`/`front.jpg` fallback in the same folder |
| **FiiO SnowSky / CP13** (retro sub-brand) | **MTP** (confirmed on Echo Mini) | Needs an MTP client on Mac despite the simple/toy styling | Device-side scan | Folder-based | FLAC/WAV/DSD/MP3 supported per FAQ | Not confirmed in sources checked |
| **HiBy R-series** | **MTP** | Needs Android File Transfer (<https://guide.hiby.com/en/docs/products/audio_player/hiby_r4/guide/>) | HiBy Music app scan, auto-creates playlists from found `.m3u` files on scan | `.m3u`/`.m3u8` import and export, plus FLAC-embedded-CUE support | Yes -- DSD/ISO/FLAC/APE/AIFF/WAV/ALAC/AAC/MP3/Vorbis/Opus | Not explicitly documented; inferred embedded + folder, Android-scanner-typical |
| **Shanling Android tier** (M3 Plus, M3/M6 Ultra) | **MTP** ("MTP USB Device" on connect) (<https://en.shanling.com/article-M6Setup.html>) | Needs MTP client | On-device scan | Folder-based, standard Android media behaviour | Yes | Standard Android scanner behaviour |
| **Shanling non-Android tier** (M1 Plus, Q1, M0 Pura) | **USB Mass Storage** (mode switch required) | Plain drive, Mac needs no driver (Windows does) (<https://headfonics.com/shanling-m1-plus-review/>) | None | Folder-based | FLAC/DSD supported | Folder-based |
| **Astell&Kern** (all current models) | **MTP only -- no mass-storage option at all** | Worst case in this document: needs OpenMTP, MacDroid, or the AK Connect app (<https://www.head-fi.org/threads/astell-kern-daps-incompatible-with-macs.972768/>) | On-device AK library/database | `.m3u` with relative paths; reported interoperability issues importing playlists built by outside tools (<https://yabb.jriver.com/interact/index.php?topic=122972.0>) | Yes | **Embedded only** -- jpeg/png embedded required, no folder.jpg fallback documented |

**The headline finding, stated plainly:** of the 16 device categories tabulated here, roughly **half
present as plain USB Mass Storage** with drag-and-drop and no desktop software at all (Rockbox'd
iPod, both non-Android Shanling tiers, Cayin N3 Pro, Sony Walkman, Innioasis, Surfans, the generic
budget tier, and Tangara), and roughly the other half use **MTP**, which is unusable on macOS
without installing Android File Transfer, OpenMTP, or MacDroid (FiiO's Android-OS M-series and its
SnowSky sub-brand, HiBy, Shanling's Android tier, iBasso). **Astell&Kern is the single worst case**:
MTP with no mass-storage fallback at all, requiring a dedicated app even by MTP standards. Almost
none of them require the computer to write a binary index the way the iPod's stock firmware does --
every device surveyed except the stock Classic either scans the filesystem itself on connect, or
(Tangara, Rockbox) has no index at all. **The iPod's `iTunesDB` requirement, which is the entire
reason saltpod exists, is the outlier in this market, not the norm.**

## 7. Honest assessment

**Is this actually a comeback, in numbers, or mostly commentary?** Thin but real, and the two
should not be conflated. What is a genuine number: Innioasis's TikTok Shop counter showing 13.9K
units sold on one SKU; Elite Obsolete Electronics' self-reported ~70 orders/day; Emily White's
70-respondent primary survey (32% Gen Z, 66% adopted within three years, 26% using an MP3 player
*instead of* streaming). What is a seller's self-reported growth claim, repeated uncritically across
2026 journalism without an independent audit: Back Market's "15.6%/year since 2022" and "117% Q4
spike," both traceable to a company blog post whose visible update date (December 2024) predates
most of the articles citing it as current. What is search-interest, not sales: the eBay
search-volume figures (+25% Classic, +20% Nano) via Axios, which could not be fetched directly to
verify. What was not found at all: any independent retail-panel (Circana/NPD-style) unit-sales
figure for MP3 players as a category, and any direct 2021-vs-2026 refurb price comparison. **The
honest summary is that the comeback is well evidenced at the anecdote-and-single-platform level and
poorly evidenced at the audited-aggregate level** -- real enough that FiiO built an entire retro
sub-brand around it and that Innioasis is selling tens of thousands of units on a single storefront,
thin enough that no outlet in this research cited a number from a source with no commercial stake
in the trend being real.

**Which devices would benefit from a saltpod-like tool, and which would not.** Would benefit: the
generic budget tier (AGPTEK/Ruizu/Mechen/Innioasis-class) has no curation tooling beyond "copy a
folder and maybe run MediaMonkey" -- a saltpod-style curated-order-plus-playlist-emitter would be a
genuine first for that tier, not an improvement on an existing one. Tangara's own documentation
says as much directly: it tells power users to hand-write playlists with `find`/`grep` and lists
"save the current queue as a playlist" as an *unbuilt* future feature -- that is close to an open
invitation. Astell&Kern's Mac story is bad enough (MTP-only) that a tool solving transfer friction
specifically would help, though its on-device scanning and native `.m3u` support mean the sync
*mechanics* are already there; the gap is Mac compatibility, not architecture. Would **not**
meaningfully benefit: FiiO/HiBy/iBasso/Shanling's Android-tier devices already have mature on-device
apps (FiiO Music, HiBy Music) that scan, auto-detect `.m3u` playlists, and extract embedded art
without any host-side tool; Sony Walkman already has native `.m3u`, FLAC, and drag-and-drop with no
Windows-only requirement for the basics. Their remaining gap -- consistent curated ordering across
more than one device -- is a smaller, more abstract win than "this device had no tooling at all."

**If saltpod were to support a second device, which one, and why.** Two defensible answers,
depending on what is being optimised for. **Optimising for market reach with the least new
engineering: a plain-USB-MSC device that already speaks native `.m3u` and FLAC** -- concretely, the
**Sony Walkman NW-A series**. The specific work required is smaller than it sounds, because almost
nothing in saltpod's current architecture is iPod-specific in spirit, only in binary format: (1) drop
the `iTunesDB` byte-surgery entirely and instead write real files into a `MUSIC/<Artist>/<Album>/`
tree, which is most of what `apply.py`'s file-placement logic already does internally before it
currently also fabricates `mhit` records; (2) emit one `.m3u` per collection with paths relative to
the Walkman's `MUSIC` folder, in the exact curated order already held in `state.collection_order`
-- this is a strictly easier target than the position-`mhod` sequencing saltpod already solved for
the iPod; (3) drop the ALAC transcoding step, since the A306 plays FLAC natively to 24-bit/384 kHz,
removing an entire pipeline stage; (4) decide an album-art convention (embedded, since Sony reads
embedded and has no documented folder.jpg fallback) and write that instead of touching an
`ArtworkDB`-equivalent, because there isn't one; (5) replace iPod GUID-based device detection with
detection of the Walkman's mount point and `MUSIC` folder structure. **Optimising for philosophical
and community fit over market reach: Tangara.** Its on-disk model -- relative-path `.m3u` text,
no index, Beets-style tag conventions, open hardware and firmware -- is closer to saltpod's own
"file on disk is master, curated order is truth" design than any other device in this document,
including the iPod. The tradeoff is scale: it is a $249 crowdfunded niche device with a community
of backers, not a consumer product with the installed base the owner would be curating libraries
for at any real volume. Sony is the pragmatic second target; Tangara is the one that would make
saltpod feel, for the first time, like it was built for a device rather than built *despite* one.
